How the score works

The score is a company's open sales roles times (1 + sales share). Sales share is the part of all its open roles that are in sales. A company with an SEC Form D filing in the last 12 months gets a 25% bonus.

A higher score means more sales openings, and more of the company's hiring going to sales.

What a Form D filing is

A Form D is the notice a private company files after selling shares, for example in a venture round. The column shows the amount sold and the month it was filed.

The amount can differ from the round a company announced, and the search misses some raises. See limitations.

Findings

What the ranking showed, and what I make of it

Observed

  • Doppel came first with 18 of its 26 openings in sales (69%), followed by 7AI with 20 of 45.
  • Of the 59 sales openings across Doppel, 7AI, and Cogent, 33 were for account executives, mostly covering U.S. regions, and just one was for an SDR.
  • Cogent had the most sales openings (21) and was the only one of the three hiring for sales ops (3).
  • Halcyon came in 17th out of 28 with six openings, three of them for account executives in Chicago, Boston, and Charlotte.
  • Only 28 of 56 companies could be ranked. The tool couldn't read Upwind's job board, Spur had no public board, and Sublime had nothing posted.

Interpretation

  • My guess is the top three already know who they're selling to and have leads coming in, so they need people who can close.
  • Cogent's sales ops hiring suggests its sales team is already big enough to need more support.
  • Halcyon probably already has people covering other cities. A company can rank low even if it already has a big sales team.

Why I'd call 7AI first

Based on what's public, Doppel looks like the safer pick. It's further along, having raised a Series C, and already has some big customers.

I'd still call 7AI first because it's the bigger bet. I'd want them to walk me through a real customer and show me what security work the AI handles on its own, where people still step in, and whether that customer renewed.

My tool also found 12 open account executive roles and three partner roles. Since 7AI says it's growing by selling through partners, I'd ask how those new reps would work with partners and how much selling they'd do directly. Source SecurityWeek

    Method

    Data snapshot

    Rationale

    Sales hiring is one of the clearest outside signs that a company has product-market fit. Sales reps are expensive, and each one has to bring in more than they cost, so a company hires a lot of them only once its product sells again and again. A company still looking for fit hires mostly engineers. So many open sales roles, especially as a share of all open roles, suggest customers are buying and the company is growing to keep up.

    I use it as a sourcing signal. It tells me which companies to look at first, not which ones are good.

    Assumptions

    • A company hires a lot of sales reps only once its product sells again and again.
    • Open roles stand in for hiring. Job postings aren't hires.
    • A job title is enough to tell a sales role from an engineering role.

    Methodology

    Companies

    The list is 56 private, venture-backed cybersecurity companies, mostly US-based, most of which announced a Series A, B or C between November 2024 and November 2026. I used an AI assistant to draft the first 50 and check them against news reports. I cut 4, added Spur, Oligo and Daylight, and added 7 more from the Form D search, which were picked for filing a raise of $5M or more in the last 12 months.

    Source

    Open roles are read from public Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby job boards.

    Tagging

    Every role is tagged Sales, Engineering or Other from its job title only (not its department). Sales Engineers, Solutions Engineers and Solutions Architects count as Sales. Sales support roles (Sales Ops, Revenue Ops, Sales Enablement, Revenue Marketing) count as Sales. Customer Success, Technical Account Manager, and brand, content, product or technical marketing roles never count as Sales.

    Score

    sales_share = sales_roles / total_roles
score       = sales_roles * (1 + sales_share)
score       = score * 1.25    (only if raised_recently)

    Form D filing

    A company gets the 25% bonus when it filed a Form D with the SEC in the last 12 months. A Form D is the notice a private company files after selling shares, for example in a venture round. The ranking shows the amount sold and the filing month from the latest filing.

    Role types

    Inside a company's panel, its sales roles are split by job title into account executives and sales reps, regional sales managers and directors, sales engineers, partners and channel, SDR and BDR, sales support and demand gen, and sales leadership. Each role is counted once, so a sales engineer who works with partners counts under partners and channel. This page does that split. The Python tool only tags Sales, Engineering or Other.

    Limitations

    • Job postings aren't hires. The tool only sees who is hiring now, not how big a team already is.
    • This tool only reads Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby job boards. 28 of the 56 companies in companies.csv aren't ranked.
    • Tagging is by title only, so a few roles land in the wrong bucket. Halcyon's "Senior Deal Desk Lead" works on pricing and contracts for sales deals, but the title has no Sales word, so it's tagged Other.
    • "None found" under Form D filing can mean the search missed it. Cogent Security, HiddenLayer, Zafran, AegisAI and Neo all announced funding in the last 12 months, but no Form D turned up under the names I searched, so none of them got the 25% bonus. With the bonus, Cogent would move up to second, ahead of 7AI.
    • I only checked announced rounds for the top 10. Companies further down could have the same gap.
    • The score is weighted toward count. A v2 could weight share more heavily, which would move focused companies like HiddenLayer up.
    • This is one snapshot. The next step is rerunning it weekly to see who is ramping up sales hiring.

Research notes

Snapshot

Findings

Observed

  • Doppel came first with 18 of its 26 openings in sales (69%), followed by 7AI with 20 of 45.
  • Of the 59 sales openings across Doppel, 7AI, and Cogent, 33 were for account executives, mostly covering U.S. regions, and just one was for an SDR.
  • Cogent had the most sales openings (21) and was the only one of the three hiring for sales ops (3).
  • Halcyon came in 17th out of 28 with six openings, three of them for account executives in Chicago, Boston, and Charlotte.
  • Only 28 of 56 companies could be ranked. The tool couldn't read Upwind's job board, Spur had no public board, and Sublime had nothing posted.

Interpretation

  • My guess is the top three already know who they're selling to and have leads coming in, so they need people who can close.
  • Cogent's sales ops hiring suggests its sales team is already big enough to need more support.
  • Halcyon probably already has people covering other cities. A company can rank low even if it already has a big sales team.
  • I'd start with 7AI, which has openings for 12 account executives and six sales engineers, because I think getting AI to do the work of an entire security operations team is harder than it sounds. I'd look at the competition and talk to customers about what they use 7AI for and whether they've kept paying for it.

Rationale

Sales hiring is one of the clearest outside signs that a company has product-market fit. Sales reps are expensive, and each one has to bring in more than they cost, so a company hires a lot of them only once its product sells again and again. A company still looking for fit hires mostly engineers. So many open sales roles, especially as a share of all open roles, suggest customers are buying and the company is growing to keep up.

I use it as a sourcing signal. It tells me which companies to look at first, not which ones are good.

Assumptions

  • A company hires a lot of sales reps only once its product sells again and again.
  • Open roles stand in for hiring. Job postings aren't hires.
  • A job title is enough to tell a sales role from an engineering role.

Methodology

Source

Open roles are read from public Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby job boards.

Tagging

Every role is tagged Sales, Engineering or Other from its job title only (not its department). Sales Engineers, Solutions Engineers and Solutions Architects count as Sales. Sales support roles (Sales Ops, Revenue Ops, Sales Enablement, Revenue Marketing) count as Sales. Customer Success, Technical Account Manager, and brand, content, product or technical marketing roles never count as Sales.

Score

sales_share = sales_roles / total_roles
score       = sales_roles * (1 + sales_share)
score       = score * 1.25    (only if raised_recently)

Raised recently

A company is marked as raised recently when it filed a Form D with the SEC in the last 12 months. A Form D is the notice a private company files after selling shares, for example in a venture round.

Limitations

  • Job postings aren't hires. The tool only sees who is hiring now, not how big a team already is.
  • This tool only reads Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby job boards. 28 of the 56 companies in companies.csv aren't ranked.
  • Tagging is by title only, so a few roles land in the wrong bucket. Halcyon's "Senior Deal Desk Lead" works on pricing and contracts for sales deals, but the title has no Sales word, so it's tagged Other.
  • A blank under Raised recently can mean the Form D wasn't found. Cogent Security shows a blank even though it announced a Series A in February 2026.
  • The score is weighted toward count. A v2 could weight share more heavily, which would move focused companies like HiddenLayer up.
  • This is one snapshot. The next step is rerunning it weekly to see who is ramping up sales hiring.
